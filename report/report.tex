\title{Bounding Visual-Inertial Odometry Drift with Self-Supervised\\
Aerial Geo-Localization for GNSS-Denied UAV Navigation}
\author{Nijat Abbasov}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Visual-inertial odometry (VIO) is locally accurate but drifts without bound;
image retrieval against satellite imagery is absolute but succeeds only
intermittently. We combine the two on CPU for GNSS-denied navigation, using
OpenVINS for motion and CAEVL --- trained on reference imagery alone --- for
absolute position. On MUN-FRL \texttt{bell412\_dataset4} (3.6\,km, Ottawa
International Airport) only 36\% of retrievals are correct, and we show over two
flights that the retrieval score is \emph{negatively} correlated with
correctness, so predictions cannot be gated individually. Rejecting outliers by
mutual consensus through the motion prior instead raises precision from 17.7\%
to 99.0\% while keeping 93\% of correct predictions. A causal
similarity-transform estimator then reduces position RMSE from $489\pm44$\,m to
$248\pm86$\,m (49\%, three runs) using only information available at each
instant. We also detect and automatically correct a 456\,m georeferencing error
in the dataset's shipped map metadata, and identify the binding limitation:
retrieval is physically possible only during the 34\% of this flight above
66\,m, since below that the camera cannot image a full map tile.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A UAV without GNSS must localise from onboard sensors. VIO integrates motion, so
its error grows without limit --- here OpenVINS \cite{geneva2020openvins} drifts
to 700\,m over 3.6\,km, 19\% of distance flown. Matching the downward camera
against georeferenced satellite imagery gives absolute position that never
drifts, but fails often: terrain must be distinctive, the aircraft high enough
to image useful ground, and the reference imagery is months or years old.

The two are complementary, which motivates fusion. The difficulty --- and the
substance of this work --- is that the retrieval output is dominated by outliers
and its confidence score cannot identify them. The contribution is therefore
about \emph{which measurements to believe}, using the motion prior rather than
appearance.

We use CAEVL \cite{caevl2025}, which trains on reference imagery alone: no
paired UAV-satellite examples, no correspondences. It operates on Canny edge
maps \cite{canny1986} to suppress the domain gap, via an autoencoder with a
DINOv2 \cite{oquab2024dinov2} perceptual loss followed by VICReg
\cite{bardes2022vicreg} fine-tuning on synthetically degraded copies. Deploying
over new terrain needs only satellite tiles.

Everything runs on CPU: OpenVINS at 20\,Hz, retrieval at 4.3\,Hz.

The scarcity is structural: VIO needs parallax, which favours low flight;
retrieval needs ground footprint, which favours high. Dataset authors optimise
for one. This tension reappears below as the dominant limitation.

We use \texttt{bell412\_dataset4}: 3.6\,km over 319\,s from a Bell 412 over
Ottawa International Airport --- nadir FLIR camera ($1440\times1080$, 20\,Hz),
Xsens MTi-30 IMU at 400\,Hz, hardware-synchronised, RTK ground truth at 5\,Hz.

\section{Data Preparation}

\subsection{IMU Timestamps}

Recorded IMU timestamps are driver \emph{receipt} times: mean interval
2500.9\,\textmu s (correct for 400\,Hz) but standard deviation
2538\,\textmu s, with gaps from 25\,\textmu s to 19\,ms. Since VIO integrates
over time, this corrupts propagation. The dataset publishes the sensor's
hardware clock on a separate topic in 1:1 correspondence; fitting an affine map
and restamping reduces the interval standard deviation to 68.5\,\textmu s, a
factor of 37, with no samples dropped.

\subsection{A 456\,m Georeferencing Error}

MUN-FRL encodes each map's georeferencing in its filename. Projecting the truth
track with those parameters places it \textbf{456\,m} from the actual flight
(Fig.~\ref{fig:trackmap}). A bounds check cannot catch this --- the track still
lands inside the image --- yet every tile then depicts different ground than it
claims. Before this was found, query and reference tiles nominally 6\,m apart
showed forest canopy and ploughed farmland respectively.

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.62\textwidth]{figures/track_on_map.png}
\caption{Red: the track projected with the published parameters. Dark curve: the
true path, drawn on the map by the dataset authors. The two differ by 456\,m.}
\label{fig:trackmap}
\end{figure*}

The authors drew the true path on each map in green, permitting automatic
recovery: we fit the scale and offset placing the known track onto that curve,
scoring by mean distance under a distance transform. Isolation is exact, since
the imagery is monochrome and only the annotation carries colour. The fit
converges to \textbf{0.35\,px (0.28\,m)}, confirming the published scale is
right and only the offsets are wrong --- by 378\,m east and 256\,m north.

\subsection{Reference Imagery}

The shipped maps remain unusable even once corrected: the painted path is
18.6\,m wide and falls inside nearly every tile a query could match, and at
0.79\,m/px they are coarser than the queries. Inpainting removes the false edge
but smears 23\% of each tile.

We instead fetch Esri World Imagery \cite{esri2026} at zoom 19 (0.21\,m/px),
2116 tiles over 6.75\,km$^2$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:coverage}). The ENU-to-pixel mapping
is solved by least squares against the PPK latitudes and longitudes, residual
\textbf{0.30\,px (0.06\,m)}. As an independent check, the PPK solution leads the
bag by exactly 18.00\,s --- the 2022 GPS-UTC leap second count.

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.62\textwidth]{figures/coverage_dataset4.png}
\caption{Left: fetched basemap (yellow) against the tiles cut from it. Right:
database size versus corridor width, 5340 tiles at 400\,m down to 1186 at
100\,m.}
\label{fig:coverage}
\end{figure*}

\subsection{Query Generation}

Tiles cover fixed ground; a frame covers area proportional to altitude. Each
output pixel is treated as a ground-plane point in ENU, rotated through the full
VIO attitude and the Kalibr \cite{furgale2013kalibr} extrinsic, then projected
through the equidistant model:
\begin{align}
\bm{P}_{\text{enu}} &= (\text{east},\ \text{north},\ z_{\text{ground}} - z_{\text{cam}}) \\
\bm{P}_{\text{cam}} &= \bm{R}_{C\rightarrow I}^{\top} (\bm{R}_{G\rightarrow I}\bm{P}_{\text{enu}} - \bm{p}_{C\ \text{in}\ I}) \\
\bm{u} &= \pi_{\text{equi}}\!\left(\bm{P}_{\text{cam},xy} / \bm{P}_{\text{cam},z}\right)
\end{align}

Full attitude matters: the aircraft banks, and this sequence sweeps
11\,422$^\circ$ of heading. An earlier version building the grid in camera axes
--- leaving queries rotated while tiles are north-up --- gave retrieval
indistinguishable from chance. Attitude must come from the VIO, since the PPK
solution is position-only.

\section{Method}

\subsection{VIO Characterisation}

OpenVINS runs monocular on the nadir camera with online calibration disabled
(the dataset supplies Kalibr values, and this trajectory does not excite every
calibration state). The camera--IMU time offset is left free and converges to
$-28.5$\,ms against the dataset's published $-29.7$\,ms, an independent check
that the configuration is right.

Anchored on the first 20\,s --- what a real vehicle has, knowing its takeoff
pose --- the estimate starts 14.7\,m from truth and drifts to 700\,m
(Fig.~\ref{fig:anchor}). A best-fit similarity reduces this to 215\,m: most of
the error is a systematic \textbf{40\% scale deficit}, not random walk. The
cause was not identified, and survived IMU restamping.

\subsection{The Retrieval Score Cannot Gate}

Over both flights, retrieval similarity is \emph{negatively} correlated with
correctness: 0.9429 for correct predictions against 0.9493 for incorrect on
\texttt{dataset4}, and 0.9447 against 0.9598 on \texttt{dataset3}. The
tile-size ablation below explains the mechanism --- descriptor collapse makes
confidence highest exactly where tiles are least distinguishable. No threshold
on the score can separate inliers; rejection must be geometric.

\subsection{Consensus Rejection}

Incorrect predictions are near-uniform over the database, so two independent
errors agreeing within $\tau$ --- after removing the VIO displacement between
their timestamps --- occurs with probability of order $\pi\tau^2/A$, about
0.5\% here ($N=1612$, $A\approx1.1$\,km$^2$, $\tau=40$\,m). Correct predictions
agree by construction. Requiring $m$ mutually consistent predictions suppresses
outliers as $(\pi\tau^2/A)^{m-1}$ while retaining inliers.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Consensus outlier rejection}
\label{alg:consensus}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require predictions $\{(t_i,\bm{f}_i)\}$, VIO $\bm{v}(t)$, window $W$,
         threshold $\tau$, support $m$
\State $\mathcal{K} \gets \emptyset$
\ForAll{windows $\mathcal{W}$ of width $W$}
  \State $best \gets \emptyset$
  \For{$n$ iterations}
    \State sample $i,j \in \mathcal{W}$; \ $s \gets \|\bm{f}_j-\bm{f}_i\|/\|\bm{v}_j-\bm{v}_i\|$
    \State $\theta \gets \angle(\bm{f}_j-\bm{f}_i) - \angle(\bm{v}_j-\bm{v}_i)$;
           \ $\bm{t} \gets \bm{f}_i - s\bm{R}(\theta)\bm{v}_i$
    \State $\mathcal{I} \gets \{k: \|s\bm{R}(\theta)\bm{v}_k+\bm{t}-\bm{f}_k\|<\tau\}$
    \If{$|\mathcal{I}|>|best|$} \State $best \gets \mathcal{I}$ \EndIf
  \EndFor
  \If{$|best| \geq m$} \State $\mathcal{K} \gets \mathcal{K} \cup best$ \EndIf
\EndFor
\State \Return $\mathcal{K}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Precision rises from 17.7\% to 99.0\% while 93\% of correct predictions

\subsection{Causal Fusion}

The VIO is mapped into the map frame by a similarity transform estimated online:
\begin{equation}
\hat{\bm{p}}_k = s\,\bm{R}(\theta)\,\bm{p}^{\text{vio}}_k + \bm{t}
\end{equation}
Estimating $s$ and $\theta$ is essential: OpenVINS initialises with arbitrary
heading, and its scale deficit would otherwise reappear between every pair of
predictions.

The estimator is strictly causal. Predictions enter a buffer as their timestamps
elapse; a \emph{trailing} window is screened for consensus; survivors
accumulate; the transform is refitted by Umeyama's method \cite{umeyama1991} to
all accepted history. Before the transform is available the estimator
dead-reckons on the VIO from the known takeoff pose --- a real vehicle has this
from a survey or the last GNSS fix --- so a position is output at every instant.
Handover occurs at $t+73$\,s.

An EKF over $(x,y,s,\theta)$ with chi-square gating was tried first and performs
worse (422\,m against 347\,m). Two reasons. \emph{Gate lockout}: a scale error
makes position error grow linearly while random-walk noise grows as
$\sqrt{\cdot}$, so the filter becomes overconfident and rejects correct
predictions --- most certain when most wrong (Fig.~\ref{fig:ekffail}).
\emph{Weak observability}: a per-prediction position update lets position absorb
the innovation, leaving $s$ to converge too slowly.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Retrieval}

Restricting the database to a corridor around positions where queries exist ---
in deployment, the motion prior's uncertainty region --- halves the tile count
and doubles recall (Table~\ref{tab:retrieval},
Fig.~\ref{fig:q200}). 83\% of erroneous predictions matched tiles that could
never have been correct.

Reducing tile size to 60\,m, to admit lower-altitude frames, \emph{degrades}
performance: at 60\,m, 2.21\% of tile pairs exceed 0.99 similarity against
0.31\% at 100\,m. Descriptors collapse, which also explains the anti-correlated
confidence score. Runtime is flat across a 3.3$\times$ database range,
confirming cost is query encoding rather than search.

\centering

\label{tab:retrieval}

\midrule

A residual error class survives any corridor: 52 incorrect predictions lie
within 100\,m of the path yet carry 398\,m median error, with a worst case of
2036\,m. These are along-track confusions --- a corridor constrains distance to
the path as a curve, not position along it --- and must be removed by the
temporal consensus test instead.

\subsection{Run-to-run Variance}

OpenVINS was run three times on identical inputs. Its dynamic initialiser starts
from assumed zero bias and solves a nonlinear problem over the first data
window, so the outcome is not deterministic (Table~\ref{tab:variance},
Fig.~\ref{fig:variance}). Runs 2 and 3 agree to within 0.1\,m and 0.002 in
scale; run 1 is a clear outlier --- a small number of distinct initialisation
basins rather than continuous noise.

The variance propagates more than proportionally: run 3's \emph{fused} error is
43\% below run 1's while its \emph{VIO} error is only 14\% below, because the
consensus test uses VIO motion to judge agreement. Better VIO gives cleaner
consensus gives a better transform.

\subsection{Computational Cost}

OpenVINS is comfortably real-time: 14.1\,ms per frame against a 50\,ms budget,
60\% of one core, 315\,MB. Retrieval is the expensive half --- 233\,ms per query
at 4.3\,Hz, but that depends on 20 threads. At 4.4 CPU-seconds per query, a
single core gives roughly 0.23\,Hz. Since fixes are consumed intermittently that
may suffice, but the multi-core figure should not be quoted alone for an
embedded computer.

\section{Limitations}

\textbf{Altitude coverage binds.} A 100\,m tile needs $\sim$66\,m of altitude,
since the camera sees about $1.5\times$ its height. Only 34\% of this flight
qualifies: takeoff, a 70\,s cruise near 105\,m, descent, then 80\,s at
$\sim$11\,m where the camera images 17\,m of ground. Running retrieval on those frames anyway was tested
and made fusion \emph{worse}, 347 to 527\,m --- 260 predictions below 66\,m were
1.9\% correct, and consensus precision fell from 87\% to 43\%. Reducing tile
size to compensate fails for the reasons above.

\textbf{Two quantities come from ground truth}: query altitude and the
VIO-to-map yaw. In deployment these come from an altimeter and a magnetometer;
MUN-FRL records neither. The VIO's own altitude is not a substitute --- median
error 29.8\,m, a 30\% error in every query's ground extent at 100\,m.

\textbf{Fusion is evaluated on one flight}, retrieval on two.

\textbf{The 40\% VIO scale deficit is unexplained}, and is also the source of
the run-to-run spread.

\section{Conclusion}

A CPU-only pipeline bounds VIO drift using self-supervised geo-localization
requiring no paired training data, on real flight data with centimetre-accurate
truth: $489\pm44$\,m to $248\pm86$\,m in real time, and 166 to 48\,m offline
where fixes exist.

The principal finding is methodological. With inlier rates below 20\% and a
confidence score anti-correlated with correctness, deciding \emph{which}
measurements to accept matters more than the estimator consuming them. Consensus
among temporally adjacent predictions, judged through the motion prior, raises
precision from 17.7\% to 99.0\% while keeping 93\% of correct predictions ---
without consulting the retrieval score or the filter's own belief, structurally
avoiding the divergence mode in which a drifted filter rejects the measurements
that would correct it.

The limiting factor is availability, not accuracy. Query-side scale
normalisation \cite{shao2026altitude}, building one database at the lowest
operating altitude and rescaling queries down, would remove both the altitude
dependence and the fixed-footprint assumption. Retraining CAEVL at a tile
footprint matched to deployment altitude --- rather than the $14.4\times$ larger
ViLD footprint --- is a training run rather than a research problem, and is the
change most likely to move the 35.8\% retrieval figure.
